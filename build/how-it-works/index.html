How to get started with a dog behaviourist

Six steps from your first message to your first consultation. Most people are through the first four within a fortnight.

Step one

Get in touch

Message me, email, or WhatsApp. Tell me your dog’s name and what has been happening.

Step two

Health and history form

A questionnaire about your dog: their background, routine, health, and what you are finding hard. It takes a little while, and it is the reason our first conversation starts somewhere useful instead of at the beginning.

Step three

Your vet’s records

With your permission I’ll contact your vet for your dog’s history. Behaviour and physical health are rarely separate, and I’d rather know before we start.

Step four

Free discovery call

Once I have read your form and your dog’s records we’ll talk for twenty to thirty minutes. If your case is outside what I do, I’ll say so and point you somewhere better.

Step five

First consultation

Ninety minutes to two hours, in person, usually at home and out on a walk. I meet your dog, film how they move, and see the behaviour where it actually happens. Afterwards you get a written Target Action Plan.

Step six

Ongoing support

The plan is the beginning, not the end. You’ll keep a diary in the app, and you can reach me between sessions.

Behaviour work happens in four stages

Complex behaviour is not solved in one session and I won’t pretend otherwise. Your plan shows which stage you are in and what has to happen before the next one. Most clients have several threads running at once, so progress on one never waits on another.

Stage one

Assessment and rule-out

What happens

I meet your dog at home and out on a walk, and film how they move. A physiotherapist reviews the footage. Anything physical goes to your vet before we go anywhere else.

What you do

Start your diary in the app. Record the ordinary days as well as the difficult ones.

What you get

Your Target Action Plan: what I saw, what I think may be going on, what would prove me wrong, and what has to happen before stage two.

Stage two

Stabilise

What happens

Changes to environment, routine and rest that take the pressure off while any veterinary work runs its course. Most early relief comes from here, and almost none of it looks like training.

What you do

Log in the app most days. This is where the diary earns its keep: stabilising is slow, and the record is how you see it working on the weeks it doesn’t feel like it is.

What you get

My comments on your entries between sessions, and an updated plan as the picture changes.

Stage three

Build

What happens

Structured work on the behaviour itself, now we know what we are building on.

What you do

Work through the exercises I send. Film your attempts. I would far rather see thirty seconds of you trying it than read a paragraph about how it went.

What you get

Exercise handouts, and my review of your videos with adjustments where something needs changing.

Stage four

Generalise

What happens

The things you actually wanted. Guests in the house. A holiday. A walk you can enjoy.

What you do

Keep recording, in the situations that used to be hard.

What you get

A comparison against stage one. The same dog, the same walk, months apart, side by side.

How long each stage takes depends on your dog. I’d rather move at their pace than promise you a timetable I can’t hold.

Common questions about dog behaviour support

Can my dog's behaviour be caused by pain?

Very often, yes. Dogs hide discomfort well, so pain shows up as behaviour long before it shows up as a limp. Reactivity on the lead, reluctance to be touched, irritability with other dogs, refusing food near a trigger and broken sleep can all have a physical driver. Every first consultation includes video of how your dog moves, reviewed by a canine physiotherapist, with findings written up for your vet.

What is the difference between a dog trainer and a dog behaviourist?

A trainer teaches your dog to do things such as recall, loose lead walking and settling. A behaviourist works on why your dog feels the way they do, which is usually what has to change first.

How much does a dog behaviourist cost?

Core support is £250 and Extended support is £400. Both include the first in-person consultation, the video and physiotherapy review, a written Target Action Plan, app access and vet liaison. Additional sessions are £100 for a 90-minute in-person visit or £25 for a 30-minute conversation. The discovery call is free.

How long does dog behaviour work take?

Longer than one session. Complex cases usually run over months rather than weeks, particularly where a physical cause needs veterinary investigation first. The work runs in four stages and your plan shows which stage you are in.

Do you use shock collars, prong collars or choke chains?

Never. Flo4Paws is a fully force-free practice — no shock, prong, choke, slip leads used as punishment, or intimidation of any kind.

Which areas do you cover?

Based in Bingham, Nottinghamshire, working across Nottinghamshire, Leicestershire and Rutland, including Radcliffe on Trent, Bottesford, Saxondale, Newark, Grantham, Melton Mowbray and Oakham.

My dog has been called aggressive. Will you still work with us?

Yes. Dogs that bark and lunge are usually frightened rather than dangerous. Some cases sit outside what I do; where they do, I will say so on the free call and point you to someone better placed, at no cost.

Do I need to see my vet first?

Not before you get in touch. If your dog has not had a check recently I will encourage one, and with your written permission I will contact your practice for their history.

WalksQuiet, low-pressure dog walks around Nottinghamshire, Leicestershire and Rutland.

Places where a nervous dog can have space, with room to give other dogs a wide berth. Decompression walks matter more than most training.

These are routes I use with clients across Nottinghamshire and Leicestershire. I know a range of areas, distances and terrain — urban and rural, short and long — so if none of these suits your dog, ask and we will find one that does.

What makes a walk good for a nervous dog is rarely the scenery. It is sight lines, so you can see who is coming; room to step aside and let something pass; and somewhere to turn back to if the day is not going well. A route that scores well on those three is worth more than a beautiful one that corners blindly.

  • Gunthorpe and the River Trent

    A quiet riverside walk near Bingham with wide paths, good visibility and room to give other dogs a wide berth. Parking, terrain and best times to go.

  • Houghton on the Hill

    Open field paths in Leicestershire with long sightlines and few blind corners, so a nervous dog sees what is coming. Parking, terrain and when to avoid it.

  • Rufford Park and Sherwood Forest

    Wide woodland trails at Rufford Park and Sherwood Forest, with space to step off the path. Busier at weekends, so go early. Parking and terrain notes.

Choosing the right one for your dog

If your dog is early in their work, start with Gunthorpe — it is 3.5 miles, flat, open, and short enough to abandon without it feeling like a failure. Houghton on the Hill has the best sight lines of the three but it is a full eight miles. Rufford and Sherwood is the most beautiful and the least forgiving: woodland means short sight lines and fast, quiet bikes.

Natural chews and why chewing calms dogsChewing releases stress, but it can also signal pain. What to offer, what to avoid, and what a sudden increase in chewing may mean.

Chewing releases stress. It is one of the most important natural activities a dog has, and for an anxious dog it is a way of bringing themselves back down without any help from you.

It is also, occasionally, a warning sign. Both things are true and it is worth knowing which you are looking at.

Why chewing calms dogs

Sustained chewing is repetitive, absorbing and self-directed. A dog with a good chew is not monitoring the window, listening to the street, or waiting for something to happen. For dogs who struggle to settle in the evening, a chew often works better than another walk.

What to offer

  • Natural single-ingredient chews — antlers, buffalo horn, dried fish skins
  • Something sized so it lasts, rather than something gone in a minute
  • Ideally, let your dog choose. Take them to the shop and see what they pick up.
  • Avoid rawhide. It swells, it is chemically processed, and it is a choking risk.

When chewing is telling you something else

A sudden increase in chewing, particularly chewing things that are not chews — post, furniture, skirting — is worth looking at rather than only managing.

Chewing is self-soothing, and dogs self-soothe when something is wrong. That can be stress, boredom or under-stimulation. It can also be discomfort. I have had cases where a dramatic increase in chewing sat alongside a gait that turned out to need veterinary attention.

If the chewing has changed recently and nothing else in the household has, do not assume it is behavioural.

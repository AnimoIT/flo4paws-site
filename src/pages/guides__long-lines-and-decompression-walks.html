Long lines and decompression walksWhy a five-metre line beats a retractable lead, where to walk a dog who cannot cope with other dogs, and how sniffing brings arousal down.

Most owners of reactive dogs walk further than they need to and let their dog sniff less than it needs to. It is an understandable mistake: a tired dog seems like a calmer dog, so the instinct is to add exercise.

For a dog whose arousal is already high, more exercise often makes things worse. What usually helps is the opposite — a shorter, slower, duller walk somewhere nothing is going to happen.

What a decompression walk actually is

Somewhere quiet and uneventful, on a long line, where your dog sets the pace and chooses where to put their nose. No ball. No training. No agenda.

It sounds like doing nothing. Physiologically it is the opposite of nothing: sniffing engages the seeking system, lowers heart rate and gives a dog something absorbing to do that has no social pressure attached to it.

Why a long line and not a retractable lead

  • A retractable lead is under constant tension, so your dog is always pulling against something. A long line is slack until it is not.
  • The handle and cord on a retractable can injure both of you if a dog bolts.
  • A long line lets you choose the length. Three metres on a path, five or more in open space.
  • You keep contact without controlling every step, which is the whole point.

I use Houndagrip lines because they are not nylon and they have grip, which matters enormously when the line is wet and your dog has just spotted a rabbit. A five-metre line is a good starting point in open ground; three metres is easier on a path.

Where to go

The best decompression spot is the boring one. Not the popular park, not the dog field at a busy hour. Somewhere with long sight lines, few blind corners, and room to step off the path and let something pass.

If your dog is ball-obsessed

Ball play is extremely arousing, and a launcher makes it worse — it requires repeated hard acceleration and sudden stopping, which puts real strain on joints.

That does not mean no ball. It means:

  • Keep the ball to one walk a day, usually the morning one
  • Throw shorter, or roll it, so there is less braking
  • Hide it in long grass so they have to search rather than sprint
  • Hide it, hold them on the lead, then release — that builds frustration tolerance
  • Bring a tug toy and let them win, so you are more interesting than the ball

What to do on the evening walk instead

Somewhere quiet, on the long line, no ball, focus on sniffing. Then feed part of the evening meal as a scatter in the grass, or in an enrichment toy at home. Both engage the seeking system and tire a dog mentally in a way that a fast walk does not.

Give it a fortnight before you judge it. Arousal comes down slowly and the first few days can look like nothing is changing. Keep a note of barking and settling in your diary — patterns show up there that you will not feel day to day.

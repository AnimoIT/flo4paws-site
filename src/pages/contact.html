The health and history form: how to get in touch

This is how you get in touch. There is no enquiry form and no waiting to hear back before anything useful happens — you fill this in, I read it, and I come back to you to arrange your free call.

It is longer than a contact form. It asks about your dog’s background, daily routine, health, diet, and what you are finding difficult. Some of it will feel like a lot of detail before we have even spoken. It is, deliberately.

I read every answer before our call, alongside your dog’s veterinary records. That means our first conversation starts with me already knowing your dog’s history rather than spending half of it catching up. And if something in there is worth raising with your vet, I can raise it in week one rather than week five.

What it covers

Step one

About you

Your name, how to reach you, and how you found me.

Step two

Your dogs

Who they are. If you have more than one, you can add them all.

Step three

About each dog

Background and history, a typical day, health and diet, your vet, and what you are finding hard. This is the long part.

Step four

Permissions

Including your permission for me to contact your vet. I need this in writing before I can speak to them — that is a legal requirement, not a preference.

Step five

Check and send

A chance to read everything back before it comes to me.

  • Set aside a bit of time and answer it somewhere quiet.
  • If you don’t know something, say so. “Not sure” is a real answer and a useful one.
  • Nothing you write is shared with anyone without your say-so.

Ready when you are

Fill it in and I’ll be in touch to arrange your free call. If your case sits outside what I do, I’ll tell you then and point you somewhere more suitable. That costs you nothing.

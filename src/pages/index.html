Signs you need a dog behaviourist, not another training class

  • Has walking your dog become a source of anxiety and stress rather than pleasure?
  • Do you dread off-lead dogs approaching yours while their owner calls out “he’s friendly”, even though yours is on a lead?
  • Has your dog been labelled aggressive or reactive for barking or lunging at other dogs or people?
  • Have strangers approached your dog without asking, then told you that you shouldn’t bring your dog somewhere if they can’t behave?
  • Are you weary of inviting family or friends to your home?

None of that means you have done anything wrong, and none of it means your dog is a bad dog.

Is your dog’s behaviour caused by pain?

I won’t start behaviour work on a dog who might be in pain. It isn’t fair on them and it doesn’t work.

Dogs are experts at hiding discomfort. In the wild, showing weakness is dangerous. A significant proportion of the cases I see have a physical component: subtle joint pain, gut trouble, a body quietly compensating for something.

Training laid on top of untreated pain does not hold. So the first job is usually to find out.

I’ll review your dog’s veterinary records, work alongside your vet, and refer to a canine physiotherapist where the picture calls for it. If it helps, I’ll come to the vet appointment with you and I won’t charge you for it.

Video gait assessment, reviewed by a canine physiotherapist

Most behaviour assessments are a conversation and an observation. Mine includes video. Slowed down and watched by the right person, that footage shows things that are invisible in the room — a shortened stride, a hip that swings wide, weight quietly shifted off one side.

Film

Short clips recorded at your consultation, at home and out on a walk.

Physiotherapy review

A canine physiotherapist assesses movement, flexion and weight-bearing, and writes back with what she sees. See who I work with.

Your vet

Those findings go into your plan as their own section, so you can hand it straight over. I’ll come to the appointment with you at no charge.

I’m a behaviourist, not a vet. I can’t diagnose anything and I won’t try. What I can do is make sure the right person is looking at the right evidence.
